# VIP onboarding playbook

The 1:1 white-glove handoff that turns a Pro/Business signup into a stuck-it-out customer. Mid-market GCs ($1M-$30M jobs) decide whether your product survives in the first 14 days. This playbook compresses that into a 45-minute call + 30 days of nudges.

The promise: by the end of the call, the customer has **one real project loaded into MAGE, an estimate generated, and the client portal sent to their actual homeowner**. If we don't get them past those three milestones in the first call, churn risk is 5×.

---

## Before the call (10 min)

1. **Pull their signup details.** Email, app version, tier (Pro vs Business), location.
2. **Look up their public website.** Note their portfolio types (custom homes? remodels? new construction?), team size, and pricing model. Lets you skip "what do you build" and jump to relevance.
3. **Pre-load a demo project that matches.** If they're a custom-home builder, seed the "Hillside Modern" demo. If they're a remodeler, seed "Jefferson Ave Renovation." Pattern-match — they'll feel seen.
4. **Confirm the calendar invite has the dial-in + a Loom backup.** Some contractors call from the truck and Zoom is flaky on a hotspot.

---

## On the call (45 min)

### Minute 0–5 · Set the stakes
Don't open with feature tour. Open with the question:

> "Walk me through what you're using right now to manage a job — from when the lead comes in to when you collect final payment."

Listen. Take notes on the SPECIFIC tools they name (QuickBooks Online, Buildertrend, Excel, the iPhone notes app, a binder in their truck). The rest of the call references these by name. **You're not selling — you're replacing.**

### Minute 5–10 · The cost surface
Add up what they just told you. Out loud. With math.

> "So that's [tool A] at $X/mo, [tool B] at $Y/mo, your office manager 4 hours a week chasing the rest of it… that's roughly $[total]/mo, plus 16 hours of admin time. MAGE Pro is $24.99. Before I show you a single feature, I want you to know that's the math we're up against."

This is the moment the customer commits internally. The features below are confirmation, not persuasion.

### Minute 10–25 · The first project (THIS IS THE WHOLE CALL)

Get them to **share their screen** (or hand them the phone) and walk them through:

1. **Create a new project** with their actual current job. Real address, real client name, real budget.
2. **Drop in a PDF of the plans** if they have one handy. Watch their face when AI Drawing Analyzer returns priced line items in 60 seconds.
3. **Generate the estimate.** Print or e-sign it. They keep the file.
4. **Open the client portal.** Send the link to their actual homeowner via SMS — right now, on the call. The homeowner will text them within an hour. That text becomes the moment the product is real to them.

If they push back ("I'll do this later"), say:

> "Doing it later means it never gets done. Five minutes here saves the trip back to your laptop tonight."

### Minute 25–35 · Their three biggest pains
Ask:

> "What's the part of running jobs that costs you the most sleep?"

Map the answer to the feature that nails it:
- "Change orders the homeowner forgets they signed" → **Open Book mode** + e-sign trail in the portal
- "Pay applications take me a whole Sunday" → **AIA G702/G703** generator
- "I'm always 2 days behind on photos and daily reports" → **Voice-to-log** + Photo Annotator
- "I don't know which job is making money" → **Cash flow dashboard** + budget vs. actual
- "I'm losing leads because I don't reply fast enough" → **AI lead scoring** + first-response timer
- "Subs ghost me on schedule updates" → **Sub portal** in Schedule Pro
- "I can never find the right plan revision" → **Plan markup** with version control + offline sync

Show ONE. Don't show all. The point is: their specific pain has a specific page.

### Minute 35–40 · Pricing + tier decision
By now they should be primed for the upgrade. Show:

- **Pro ($24.99/mo)** — owner-operator, 1-3 active projects, AI takeoff (Standard), 8 of the 8 stack tools.
- **Business ($63.99/mo)** — small office, unlimited projects, AI takeoff (Pro Estimator depth, 5–10× richer reads), priority AI flows, custom branding.

Decision rule: "If you have an office manager OR more than 4 active projects OR you do bid-to-spec commercial work, Business pays for itself in one accurate takeoff. Otherwise Pro."

Don't push. The customer paying $24.99 happily for 18 months is worth more than the customer who upgrades grudgingly and churns at month 3.

### Minute 40–45 · The 14-day check-in
Set their expectations:

> "I'll text you on day 7 to see how the first project's going. If the AI estimate missed something, I want to fix the prompt — not just yours, every customer's. On day 14 we'll look at whether your homeowner is opening the portal. If not, we'll figure out why."

This converts "support" into "co-builder" — they feel like they're shaping the product, not buying it.

## After the call

**Day 0** — Send a recap email with:
- Their first project link
- The portal link they sent the homeowner
- 3 short Loom videos (≤90s each) addressing their three biggest pains
- A direct text number to reach you

**Day 1** — Text: "Did the homeowner open the portal yet?"

**Day 3** — Text: "Want me to walk through pay app generation? Takes 4 min."

**Day 7** — Text: "How's the first project? Anything I can fix in the AI?"

**Day 14** — Text: "Quick 10-min check-in this week?" Book a Calendly slot.

**Day 30** — Email: "You've used MAGE on [N] projects so far. Here's what your usage looks like vs. our top 10% of customers." Surfaces gaps without selling.

## Red flags during the call

| Signal | What it means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| "I don't have a current project handy" | They're tire-kicking; not paying | Politely end at minute 25, send the recap email, expect churn |
| "I'll have my partner/admin look at this" | Not the decision-maker | Get the partner's email, schedule a follow-up with both |
| "Can you also do [unrelated thing]?" | Wrong-fit prospect | Be honest. Suggest the closest fit. Better than upselling and churning. |
| "I tried Buildertrend / Procore and hated it" | Strong fit signal | Lean in: "What specifically did you hate?" — every answer maps to a MAGE feature |
| "I don't really do email" | Field-first GC, perfect ICP | Lean in: "Then this app is built for you. We send everything via SMS." |

---

## What NOT to do

- **Don't demo Settings.** Settings doesn't sell anything.
- **Don't show every feature.** Show three. Three is the right number.
- **Don't read off the marketing site.** They already read it. They're past that.
- **Don't promise features that aren't shipped.** "Coming FY26" doesn't help today's job.
- **Don't take more than 45 minutes.** Past 45 you're stealing from their day, and they'll resent the product for it.

## Metrics that matter

Track per call:
- **Time-to-first-project-created** (target: ≤8 min from connect)
- **Time-to-first-AI-estimate** (target: ≤15 min)
- **Portal sent to homeowner during call** (target: 80%+ of calls)
- **Day-7 retention** (target: 90% still active)
- **Day-30 paid retention** (target: 80% still on the same tier)

If any number drops, the call structure has a leak.
