# MAGE ID — polish audit, 2026-09-10

First audit of this product made against what it ACTUALLY RENDERS. All 107 registered
routes were mounted in the real provider stack with a seeded session, in an empty
account and a populated one, and their visible text captured. Every finding below
quotes that text and names the cause in source. Each dimension was then attacked by a
verifier whose job was to catch invented quotes and inflated severity — several
findings were refuted or downgraded and those corrections are kept.

Evidence: scratchpad/routes-empty.txt, scratchpad/routes-populated.txt

---

## THE MONEY SCREENS — job costing, WIP, cash flow, invoicing, AIA, change orders, retention, reports, budget, estimate output, buyout, 1099, profit leak

**The labels are better than almost anything else in this app — cost and revenue are distinguished in words, negatives are phrased correctly, and /tax-1099-export is a masterclass in honest scoping. But the screen literally titled "Bank-Ready Reports" prints a $18,530 LOSS on a job carrying $23,670 of real margin, because a free-text "Phase (optional)" field whose placeholder is "Electrical" makes the app double-count every sub you buy out. And on the same seeded account, /wip-report says +15% margin while /reports says -11.9%. That is the thing a contractor would have to explain away.**

### What is good

- The money-screen empty states are the strongest copy in the product, and they are NOT dead ends. /job-costing empty: "No projects yet | Job costing rolls up commitments, receipts, crew hours and change orders for one project at a time. Create a project first so Job Costing has somewhere to land. | 1 Open or create a project from the Projects tab. | 2 Inside the project, log a sub commitment, invoice, or change order. | 3 Tap Job Costing in the project tile grid to see budget vs. actual. | Create a project". Same numbered-3-step-plus-CTA shape on /invoice, /aia-pay-app, /change-order, /budget-dashboard, /lien-waivers, /portfolio-margin, /margin-alerts, /payments. A trialing contractor who lands on a money screen on day one is told what the screen is for, what data it needs, and where to go next. Do not let anyone talk you into 'improving' these.
- /tax-1099-export is the honesty high-water mark of the whole app, and it is the standard the accounting screens should be held to. It renders its own blind spot before you ask: "What this counts | Counts sub-portal invoices marked paid — checks, ACH, cash and anything else recorded outside the portal are not in this figure" and then "MAGE ID isn't a tax-prep tool. We don't file 1099s for you, don't verify TIN matches, and don't compute backup withholding. Hand this CSV to your CPA". It also states its own threshold inline ("paid ≥ $2,000 for 2026") rather than making the number arrive unexplained.
- Negative and deductive cases are phrased correctly everywhere I checked — the 'plus $-30,000' shape does not recur on the money surfaces. app/buyout.tsx:286-292 flips the LABEL, not just the sign: `kpi.savingsToDate >= 0 ? 'Savings' : 'Overrun'` and `{kpi.savingsToDate >= 0 ? '+' : ''}`, repeated per-package at :365-367. app/change-order.tsx:691/699/705 guard the '+' the same way; :1313-1315 says "Credits $X back to the contract" vs "Commits $X to the contract". app/wip-report.tsx:754-758 writes "less $30,000 of approved deductive change orders" instead of "plus $-30,000". app/cash-flow.tsx:855 only renders its '+' prefix behind a `committed.undated > 0` guard. This is a solved problem, not a lurking class.
- /wip-report teaches its own vocabulary on screen instead of assuming the reader is an accountant: "Over = billed more than earned (client is ahead of you) · Under = earned more than billed (you are financing the client)". That parenthetical is the single best sentence on any money screen here — it converts a term of art into a cash-position statement a contractor actually feels.
- Cost is distinguished from revenue in the WORDS, not just the code, on every labelled surface I read. /reports Profit tab: "Revenue | Cost to date | Est. final cost | Projected profit" (app/reports.tsx:310-315). And the separation is defended at build time — utils/jobCostEngine.ts:299-306 accepts `invoices` and deliberately never reads it ("Client invoices are REVENUE"), with scripts/validate-money-definitions.ts failing the build if an invoice payment moves `summary.actual`. The bug you fixed today has a regression guard behind it.
- /cash-flow refuses two easy lies. It names what its own headline number is NOT: "Net Cash Change | $0 | Cash in minus cash out. Not profit — excludes unbilled work." And it surfaces money it could not honestly place on a week rather than dropping it: "+ $42K committed on jobs with no schedule or already finished — not in the weeks above" (app/cash-flow.tsx:857). Most tools would have silently omitted that $42K.
- The estimate wizard's output does not present an estimate as a measurement. utils/groundingChip.ts:62 — when the contractor has no cost history the chip reads "Priced from market averages — MAGE has none of your rates yet", and app/estimate-wizard.tsx:847-852 omits the confidence pill AND meter entirely rather than defaulting to 70 ("No `?? 70`"). It then hands over the fix instead of just confessing: "Price this from your numbers — Paste or type the rates you already charge". This is the pattern the WIP and Reports screens are missing.
- The exported PDF carries the caveat the screen omits — utils/financialReportPdf.ts:68: "Estimates as of report generation date. Final costs subject to change as commitments are placed and invoices settle." The artifact that actually reaches the banker is more honest than the screen the contractor reads.
- The WIP provenance work is real, not aspirational. app/wip-report.tsx:743-770 renders "Where these numbers come from" with a plain-English line per figure, and utils/wip.ts:187 even has a label for the null case ("No source on file — enter this figure yourself") plus utils/wip.ts:275 for pre-provenance snapshots ("Not recorded — this snapshot predates source tracking"). The row list also distinguishes "· entered by you, synced" from "· entered here, not synced yet" — a distinction almost no competitor makes.

### P0

**/reports** _(medium)_

> Bank-Ready Reports | WIP TOTAL — 1 project | Revised contract $155,172 | Billed $0 | Retainage held $0 | Projected profit -$18,530 | Harlow Residence — kitchen + primary suite | -11.9 % | Contract $155,172 | Approved COs $0 | Revised $155,172 | % Complete 0% | Billed $0 | Paid $0 | Retainage $0 | Est. final cost $173,702 | Projected profit -$18,530

The seeded job has a $155,172 contract and a $131,502 cost estimate — a real +$23,670 / +15.25% margin. The screen titled "Bank-Ready Reports" instead prints "Est. final cost $173,702" and "Projected profit -$18,530" on a job that is 0% complete with $0 of cost actually recorded. The $42,200 gap is the two subcontracts the contractor signed for scope that IS in his estimate — they are added to the cost-at-completion ON TOP of the estimate lines they replace. The screen offers no caveat, no provenance, and no hint that anything is unbudgeted. A contractor who buys out his job — the single most normal thing a GC does — watches his only profit report invert.

- **Cause:** utils/jobCostEngine.ts:414 seeds each budget phase from the estimate item's `category` (the fixture's two sub lines are category 'subcontractor', $18,400 + $22,600 = $41,000). utils/jobCostEngine.ts:467-471 then adds commitments under `commitmentPhase(c)` — the free-text `Commitment.phase` ('Plumbing', 'Electrical'). Because the strings differ, each commitment creates a NEW phase whose budget is 0, and the EAC at :623 is per-phase `actual + remainingCommitted + max(0, budget - committed - direct)`: the 'subcontractor' phase still projects its full $41,000 (nothing was committed against it) while 'Electrical' and 'Plumbing' project $22,600 + $19,600 on top. 131,502 + 42,200 = 173,702, and 155,172 - 173,702 = -18,530 (-11.94% → "-11.9 %"). Had the phase strings matched, the EAC would be $132,702 and the profit +$22,470. The engine knows: classify() at utils/jobCostEngine.ts:286 returns 'unbudgeted' and its comment names this exact cause — "a commitment tagged with a phase name the estimate spells differently". Only app/job-costing.tsx:660 ever renders that word; app/reports.tsx:257-261 and the whole of /wip-report throw the honesty away and print the poisoned total. And the app hands the contractor the poison: app/job-costing.tsx:841-842 is `<Field label="Phase (optional)"><TextInput ... placeholder="Electrical" />` — free text, no picker, no match against the estimate's categories, and its placeholder is literally the string that breaks it.
- **Fix:** Two parts, ship the honesty half first. (1) Small: make unbudgeted cost visible wherever EAC is rolled up. `computeJobCost` already has `byPhase[].status === 'unbudgeted'` — sum those phases' projectedFinal into a new `unbudgetedProjected` on JobCostSummary, expose it on WIPRow, and render it on app/reports.tsx (both WIP and Profit tabs) as its own line: "Unbudgeted committed cost $42,200 — money on phases your estimate never priced. Retag these to a budgeted phase or your projected profit is understated by this amount." Do the same in utils/financialReportPdf.ts. (2) Medium, the real fix: replace the free-text phase input at app/job-costing.tsx:841-842 with a picker seeded from `project.linkedEstimate` item categories (plus 'Other…' free text), and on save normalise case/whitespace. Then add a reconciliation nudge on /job-costing for any existing commitment sitting in an unbudgeted phase ("Electrical — $22,600 committed, no budget. Is this the 'subcontractor' line in your estimate?").

**/wip-report vs /reports** _(medium)_

> /wip-report: "Revised contract | $155,172 | ... | Weighted margin | 15%"  —  /reports: "Revised contract $155,172 | ... | Projected profit -$18,530 | ... | -11.9 %"

Same account, same session, same single project, same $155,172 contract — and the two bank-facing money screens disagree about whether the contractor is making 15% or losing 11.9%. Neither hero says which cost basis it used, so there is no way to tell from the screens which one to believe. This survives fixing the finding above: they would then read +15.25% and +14.5%, still not footing, because they are two independent cost engines. If a banker or accountant is handed both exports — and /reports advertises "Export to CSV or PDF in one tap" while /wip-report has "Export CSV | Export PDF" — the contractor is the one who has to explain the contradiction.

- **Cause:** Two cost-at-completion definitions. /wip-report goes through utils/wip.ts:478-502 `deriveEstimatedCostWithSource`, which takes `linkedEstimate.baseTotal` ($131,502) and, by design, never looks at commitments when an estimate exists → margin (155,172-131,502)/155,172 = 15.25% → "15%". /reports goes through utils/financialReports.ts:143 `estimatedFinalCost = job.projectedFinal` from the jobCostEngine, which is commitment-driven ($173,702). Both are defensible in isolation; nothing reconciles them, and neither app/wip-report.tsx:603 (`<Row label="Weighted margin" .../>`) nor app/reports.tsx:231-234 names its basis.
- **Fix:** Pick one definition of estimated-cost-at-completion and route both screens through it — `deriveEstimatedCostWithSource` is the better citizen because it already returns provenance, so extend it to blend in commitments (max of estimate-base and committed+uncommitted-remainder, per phase) rather than adding a third. Until that lands, print the basis under each margin as one muted line: on /wip-report "Margin from your estimate's cost basis ($131,502) — signed commitments not yet reflected"; on /reports "Margin from committed + projected cost ($173,702)". Two screens that disagree but each say why are survivable; two that disagree silently are not.

### P1

**/cash-flow** _(trivial)_

> Cash Flow | When will money come in? | ... | Current Balance | $0 | Healthy | ... | Total Income $0 | Total Expenses $0 | + $42K committed on jobs with no schedule or already finished — not in the weeks above | Net Cash Change $0 | ... | Cash Flow Setup | 1 | /4 | Current Bank Balance | This is your starting point.

A green check and the word "Healthy" sit next to a $0 bank balance — on a screen that is simultaneously telling the contractor it has not been set up yet ("Cash Flow Setup 1/4"), and that has $42,200 of signed subcontracts it could not place on a week. "Healthy" is not a reading of his business; it is an artifact of every input being zero. This is a default presented as a fact, and it is on the one screen a contractor opens when he is worried about money. It renders identically in the empty and populated dumps, which is how you know no data is reaching it.

- **Cause:** app/cash-flow.tsx:353-357. The ladder is `forecast.length === 0 → 'Setup'`, `lowestBalance < 0 → 'Danger'`, `netCashChange < 0 → 'Watch'`, else `'Healthy'`. With a $0 starting balance and no dated rows, `forecast.length` is non-zero (12 empty weeks are generated), and lowestBalance and netCashChange are both exactly 0 — neither strictly negative — so it falls through to 'Healthy' with a CheckCircle at :651.
- **Fix:** Add a branch before 'Healthy' in app/cash-flow.tsx:353: if no starting balance has been recorded (the setup wizard is still open) OR every forecast week is zero, return the existing `'neutral'` / 'Setup' shape rather than 'Healthy'. Cheaper still and more useful: when `committed.undated > 0` and the forecast is empty, label it "Not forecast yet — $42K committed with no dates". Never let an all-zero input render as a positive verdict.

**/reports** _(trivial)_

> Harlow Residence — kitchen + primary suite | -11.9 | % | Contract | $155,172

A bare "-11.9 %" pill sits against the project name with no label at all. On a screen that also shows "% Complete 0%", a reader — the contractor's accountant included — cannot tell whether that pill is margin, completion, variance, or cost overrun. The app already knows how to say it properly 40 lines away: the Profit hero at app/reports.tsx:284 renders "15.3% margin". The per-project pills just never got the noun.

- **Cause:** app/reports.tsx:243-244 (WIP tab) and app/reports.tsx:304-305 (Profit tab) both render `{r.projectedMargin.toFixed(1)}%` with no accompanying text, styled only by colour tone. Colour is carrying the whole meaning, and colour is exactly what does not survive a screenshot in an email or a black-and-white print.
- **Fix:** Append the noun in both places: `{r.projectedMargin.toFixed(1)}% margin`, matching app/reports.tsx:284. If the pill is too narrow, add an `accessibilityLabel` and put "Projected margin" as a tiny caption above the pill — but a number this consequential should not ship unlabelled to save eight pixels.

**/wip-report** _(small)_

> Revised contract | $155,172 | Earned revenue | $0 | Billed to date | $0 | Overbilling | $0 | Underbilling | $0 | Backlog | $155,172 | Weighted margin | 15% | ... | Export CSV | Export PDF

The provenance work landed, but it does not reach the figures a banker actually reads. The portfolio hero — the seven numbers at the top, including the 15% margin and the $155,172 backlog — carries no source line, and "Export CSV / Export PDF" sit directly beneath it. The "Where these numbers come from" panel exists and is excellent, but it is one level down, behind tapping an individual project row, and it covers the per-project drill figures only. The dimension's question — does the Source work reach the screen? — answers: it reaches a modal, not the report.

- **Cause:** app/wip-report.tsx:596-603 renders the portfolio hero as plain `<Row label=... value=... />` with no `sources` reference; the provenance block is at :743-770, inside the `drillProjectId !== null` Modal. `describeWipRowSources` (utils/wip.ts:293) is wired to the export path but nothing renders a basis at portfolio level. /reports has no provenance at all, on screen or otherwise — only its PDF carries a generic disclaimer (utils/financialReportPdf.ts:68).
- **Fix:** Add one muted line under the /wip-report hero naming the dominant cost basis across rows, e.g. "Cost basis: your estimate's pre-markup total for 1 of 1 project. Tap a project for the source of every figure." Reuse `WIP_SOURCE_LABELS`; when rows disagree on basis, say "mixed — tap each project". Then do the same on app/reports.tsx:146 under "Bank-Ready Reports". A screen may not call itself bank-ready and also decline to say where its numbers came from.

### P2

**/wip-report** _(trivial)_

> Harlow Residence — kitchen + primary suite | 0% complete · $0 earned | Cost-to-date | $0 | · est. (tap to add labor) | Under $0

"$0 · est." calls a zero an estimate. Nothing was estimated — nothing has been recorded. On this row it is worse than vague: the same account holds $42,200 of signed subcontracts, so a contractor reading "Cost-to-date $0 · est." reasonably concludes the app has estimated his cost at zero, rather than that no payment has cleared yet. The parenthetical "(tap to add labor)" also asks for labor specifically when the honest ask is broader.

- **Cause:** app/wip-report.tsx:673-680. The three-way branch on `r.sources?.costToDate` falls to the literal `' · est. (tap to add labor)'` for the auto-derived case regardless of whether the derived value is a real figure or zero. `suggestCostToDateWithSource` (utils/wip.ts:436-448) returns `committed + materials` where `committed` is the sum of `paidToDate` — correctly $0 here, since nothing has been paid — but the label does not distinguish "we computed a lower bound" from "we found nothing".
- **Fix:** Branch on the value as well as the source in app/wip-report.tsx:676. When `r.input.costToDate === 0` and there is no override, print "Cost-to-date $0 — nothing paid out yet ($42,200 committed). Tap to enter your real cost." using the `auto.committed` / `auto.materials` split the drill panel already computes. When it is non-zero, keep the existing lower-bound wording but say what it excludes, as the drill panel does.

### P3

**/buyout** _(trivial)_

> Harlow Residence — kitchen + primary suite | Bought out | 0 | % | $0 | of | $0 | Savings | + | $0 | vs. estimate | Packages | 0 / 0 | 0 open | 0 lvl | Scope packages | No packages yet

"$0 of $0" is a denominator the app could fill in. This project has a $131,502 cost estimate and $42,200 of subcontracts already signed, so "0% bought out, $0 of $0" both understates what is known and reads like a broken calculation. The empty state below ("Create a scope package — Plumbing rough-in, Drywall, MEP, etc.") rescues it, so this is cosmetic rather than misleading — but it is a zero where a real number exists.

- **Cause:** app/buyout.tsx:114-118 derives `totalBudget` and `committedBudget` purely from `packages` (`packages.reduce((s, p) => s + p.estimateBudget, 0)`), so with no packages both are 0 and `pctBoughtOut` short-circuits to 0. The project's own estimate and existing commitments are never consulted for the denominator.
- **Fix:** When `packages.length === 0`, render the tile against what is known instead of against nothing: denominator from the estimate's subcontracted-category cost, numerator from signed commitments — "$42,200 of $131,502 estimated cost · 0 packages created". Failing that, suppress the "$0 of $0" sub-line entirely while the package list is empty; the empty state is already carrying the message.

## Empty states and honesty — does the app tell the truth when it has nothing?

**The empty-state system is genuinely strong — roughly 60 of 107 routes explain themselves and name the prerequisite, and the "failed read looks like no data" trap the last audit feared is already fixed on the home attention card. The rot is narrow and specific: six screens compute a SUCCESS VERDICT from absent data, and three of them are money screens. The worst is not /wip-report's zeros — it is that the first card on the home screen says "All clear — your jobs are on track" and the morning brief says "Nothing overdue, nothing at risk" while an RFI sits 23 days overdue two inches below, because the attention engine has no RFI category at all.**

### What is good

- The prerequisite-empty-state pattern is excellent and applied consistently across ~35 routes. /portfolio-margin: "No margin to roll up yet | The Margin Board ranks every active project by margin risk. To populate it: 1 Mark projects as estimated or in progress. 2 Give each an estimate with markup so there is a margin to track. 3 Award buyout and approve change orders — the board updates live. | Open Projects". That is a real empty state: it explains the screen, names the exact preconditions in order, and gives the action. /margin-alerts, /estimate-calibration, /sub-scorecard, /cost-database, /payments, /report-inbox, /documents, /lien-waivers, /photo-triage, /budget-dashboard and ~20 more all follow it. Most products do not have one screen this good.
- The audit's stated most-dangerous theme — "a failed read renders as 'you have no data'" — is ALREADY FIXED on the highest-traffic surface. components/home/BrainWatchCard.tsx:96-110 distinguishes them explicitly: "RT-R1: a failed fetch (dead session, no network) yields the same total === 0 as a genuinely quiet book — a dead session once showed a green all-clear while every read 401'd. When MAGE could not be reached, say so", rendering "Couldn't reach MAGE — showing what's on this phone" instead of the all-clear. Credit where due.
- /cost-database is the model every other money screen should copy. Populated it renders "YOU SET THIS · NOT YET MEASURED | $78.00 | ± 0 %" per rate, "Bid accuracy | — | see what it costs you", and "Brain accuracy | The brain is grading itself — first results after your predictions resolve." It distinguishes measured-zero from not-yet-measured on every single figure, uses an em-dash rather than a fake 0%, and labels provenance per row. /cost-seed adds "A job count you type is your own note, not evidence" and "Rates you enter are marked 'you set this' — never counted as a closed job, never folded into your bid accuracy." This is exactly the discipline the rest of the app needs; it already exists in-house.
- /business already has the right primitive for cold-start metrics: "CRM Win Rate | — | 3 more decided leads needed" and "Bid Win Rate | — | 3 decided bids needed" (ColdStartNote, app/business.tsx:103, 264-286), plus "Closes feed this — 0 of 2 jobs needed for your first type comparison" and "Weather patterns build from your daily reports — 0 filed so far." It never prints a computed-looking number it cannot justify.
- /wip-report has had real honesty work done on it despite the zeros problem: a full explainer sheet defining earned vs billed and why a surety asks about underbilling, an on-screen legend ("Over = billed more than earned (client is ahead of you) · Under = earned more than billed (you are financing the client)"), and per-row provenance on the input that matters — "Cost-to-date $0 · entered by you, synced" vs "entered on this device" vs the estimate, with the code comment "the GC only found out which he had when the surety asked."
- Several screens correctly refuse to overstate their own authority: /safety-osha — "Verify against your recordkeeping before posting."; /tax-1099-export — "What this counts | Counts sub-portal invoices marked paid — checks, ACH, cash and anything else recorded outside the portal are not in this figure" plus "MAGE ID isn't a tax-prep tool. We don't file 1099s for you"; /delay-events — "It is not legal advice — check your contract" and "Recording a notice here is not the same as sending it."; /cash-flow — "Net Cash Change | Cash in minus cash out. Not profit — excludes unbilled work." and the honest exclusion footnote "+$42K committed on jobs with no schedule or already finished — not in the weeks above".
- The day-one home screen is a genuinely good trial experience, not a dead end: "Get up and running | 5 quick steps. About 2 minutes." with five concrete steps, then "Build something | Your first project is one tap away... | Create your first project | Try a sample project (small or large)". Offering a sample project on an empty account is the right call and most competitors do not.

### P0

**/brief and /(tabs)/(home)** _(medium)_

> POPULATED /brief: "Thursday, Sep 10 | Quiet morning — nothing needs you | Nothing overdue, nothing at risk, nothing waiting on you. Go build."  —  POPULATED /(tabs)/(home), same session, same screen: "Your morning brief | Quiet morning — nothing needs you | All clear — your jobs are on track. | ... | RFI #2 past due · 23d | Shower niche waterproofing detail · Harlow Residence — kitchen + primary suite"

The app asserts three checked negatives — "Nothing overdue, nothing at risk, nothing waiting on you" — on an account that has a Request For Information 23 days past due to the architect. The home screen prints the all-clear and the overdue RFI within one scroll of each other. /waiting-on, reading the same data, correctly says "1 item overdue | 1 over a week late." So the data is there and one screen finds it; the two surfaces that issue the verdict do not. This is category (c) and (d) at once: on an empty account it is a green verdict on nothing, and on a populated account it is a false verdict on real data. It is the FIRST card on the home screen, so it is what a trialing contractor reads before anything else — and "nothing needs you" is the single claim most likely to cost them money if wrong.

- **Cause:** utils/brainWatch.ts:26 — `export type AttnKind = 'schedule' | 'invoice' | 'permit' | 'cert' | 'closeout' | 'punch' | 'changeOrder' | 'delivery' | 'buildingAccess'`. There is no `rfi` and no `submittal` kind, and no rfiAttention/submittalAttention function exists. Consequently: hooks/useBrainWatch.ts:74-84 aggregates only schedule/invoice/permit/closeout/cert/punch/changeOrder → total===0 → components/home/BrainWatchCard.tsx:112-119 renders "All clear — your jobs are on track." And utils/brief/composeBrief.ts contains zero occurrences of "rfi" (grepped) → briefIsEmpty() at composeBrief.ts:408-410 returns true → app/brief.tsx:167-172 renders the green CheckCircle2 card. Worse, the two all-clear claims are computed from DIFFERENT partial sets: useBrainWatch includes punch + changeOrder but not delivery/buildingAccess; composeBrief imports delivery/buildingAccessAttention but not punch/changeOrderAttention. Neither includes RFIs or submittals.
- **Fix:** Add `rfi` and `submittal` to AttnKind and write rfiAttention()/submittalAttention() in utils/brainWatch.ts (overdue-by-deadline is the same shape as invoiceAttention; hooks/useSmartInbox.ts:195 already computes the exact line — "RFI #${rfi.number} past due · ${daysLate}d" — so the logic can be lifted). Then make ONE canonical attention set and have both useBrainWatch and composeBrief consume it, so home and /brief can never disagree. Until the categories are complete, scope the copy to what was actually checked: "Nothing overdue in schedules, invoices, permits or certs" is defensible; "Nothing overdue, nothing at risk, nothing waiting on you" is not. ## Dead ends, error copy, and the words themselves

**The empty states are genuinely good — better than most shipped software — but the app's confident lines are its worst ones: on the same home screen it says "All clear — your jobs are on track" ten rows above "RFI #2 past due · 23d", and the paywall's only sentences are "Upgrade Required" and "In-app purchasing is currently unavailable."**

### What is good

- The empty-world states are a real asset, not a liability. Roughly 30 screens use the same numbered "here is how to populate this" pattern and it works: /cost-database — "Your cost database learns what work actually costs from every job you close — which means it has nothing on day one. - The honesty vocabulary around learned costs is the best writing in the product and it is exactly the right instinct: "YOU SET THIS · NOT YET MEASURED", "Rates you enter are marked \"you set this\" — never counted as a closed job, never folded into your bid accuracy. The first real job you close corrects them", "A job count you type is your own note, not evidence". Nothing else in construction software distinguishes a stated rate from a measured one in the UI. Protect this.
- /waiting-on in the populated world is the single best screen in either dump: "1 item overdue | 1 over a week late. | Each one has a follow-up written and ready — send it without typing. | RFI #2: Shower niche waterproofing detail · with Architect · 23d late" — then the actual drafted follow-up sentence, then "Send follow-up". It is a route, not a wall. /nearby-rfps' "Browsing nearby projects is coming soon" is also correct — it is flag-gated deliberately (RFP_BROWSE_ENABLED) and offers an alternative, and its own source comment says the previous copy was "a permanent lie". ## The first ten minutes — what a trialing contractor actually sees

**The empty-state layer is the best-built thing in this app — every screen a new user lands on names itself, explains why it's blank, and hands him one action. The damage is in the funnel wrapped around it: the pricing page sells "Unlimited Projects" on Free while the app blocks project #2, the 8-step estimate wizard charges 3 mandatory Next-taps that cannot block, the empty home hides both conversational surfaces that differentiate the product, and the 2-lifetime AI-estimate allowance is spent invisibly.**

### What is good

- The empty-state layer is genuinely excellent and, unusually, CONSISTENT. Fifteen-plus screens follow the same contract: name the feature, say why it's empty in the contractor's language, then one CTA. /ai-punch: "No projects yet | AI Punch turns walkthrough photos into a punch list — pick photos, AI drafts the items, you review and save them into the project. Create a project first so AI Punch has somewhere to land. | Create a project". /cost-database goes further and teaches the cold start: "Your cost database learns what work actually costs from every job you close — which means it has nothing on day one. Don't wait six months for it: 1 Seed the rates you already know…". This is better than most shipped construction SaaS and it is the reason a trial does not die on the second screen.
- The onboarding arc puts value before the paywall, on purpose and in writing. app/onboarding.tsx runs splash → 5 preview cards → paste-your-rates → /estimate-wizard?onboarding=1, and the rates step is honest about why it's asking: "MAGE learns your rates from every job you close — which means nothing to price with today." It has a real escape ("I'll add rates later") and it labels what it saves: "Saved as rates you set — never counted as closed jobs." That is the correct answer to the cold-start problem and it does not pretend.
- /persona-select — the actual first screen after sign-up — does its job in one look: "What brings you here?" plus "MAGE ID has two sides — the operating system for builders, and a marketplace for property owners hiring them. Pick one and we'll set up the right experience. You can switch later in Settings." Four labelled cards with concrete blurbs and a reassurance line. Nothing to fix here.
- Tools (app/(tabs)/discover/tools.tsx) gates 33 of its 53 rows behind `needsProjects` and then SAYS SO: "More tools unlock with projects | Most tools (Daily reports, Compliance, Closeout, Reporting) are project-aware. Create your first project to unlock them." Hiding rows and explaining the hiding is the right pair; most apps do one or the other.
- The bid-identity gate (utils/bidDocumentIdentity.ts:167) is the best-written block in the codebase: title "This prints on the homeowner's copy", reason "The header of this PDF prints your company name. With it blank the proposal goes out as 'MAGE ID' — the software's name, not yours." A blocked button that explains itself in terms of what the homeowner will be holding.
- The AI-limit wall is honest and escapable: "You've seen what it can do" / "You've used your 2 free AI Estimate trials. Upgrade to Pro for unlimited use." with "See plans" and "Not now" (components/UpgradeSheet.tsx:33-37).
- The estimate wizard never silently dead-ends. A blocked Next sets `stepBlockReason` — "Enter the size with a number — e.g. 2500 or 2,500 sqft." — and `firstNumber()` tolerates "2,500", "1500 sqft", "6-8 weeks" (utils/scopeQuestions.ts:84-106).
- The iOS push prompt is deliberately never spent during onboarding: "Never during onboarding — the flow is still mid-arc and a system dialog there spends the one iOS prompt cold" (app/estimate-wizard.tsx:553-557). Correct and rare.
- Empty is distinguished from broken. `sourceFailed` splits "brand-new account" from "every read 401'd" so a reinstalling GC gets "Couldn't reach MAGE | Your projects didn't come back from the last read. Nothing has been deleted" instead of day-one onboarding copy (app/(tabs)/(home)/index.tsx:990-1003, app/(tabs)/summary/index.tsx:191).
