# App experience audit — 2026-09-07

Ten parallel auditors across visual craft, empty/loading/error states, navigation,
onboarding, the core GC workflow, field ergonomics, AI honesty, money surfaces,
feature gaps and copy. Every dimension's findings were then attacked by a separate
adversarial verifier told to refute them and to default to dropping when uncertain.

**108 findings survived. 35 were refuted (24%). 70 were reclassified** —
mostly impact downgrades and corrected mechanisms, not upgrades. The raw audit
overstated severity; what is below is what survived.

## One claim I verified myself, because it changes how you read CI

The report says the guards are green while the defects they exist to catch are
shipping. That is serious enough that I did not take it on trust:

- `app/accept-invite.tsx:123` sets `btnText` to `color: '#FFF'`, rendered on
  `backgroundColor: t.accent` (`#FF6A1A`) at :85 and :96.
- My own WCAG relative-luminance calculation for white on `#FF6A1A`: **2.87:1**.
  AA needs 4.5:1 for normal text and 3.0:1 even for large text.
- `bun run test:contrast` **exits 0 — PASS.**

So a below-AA button ships on the screen a new collaborator sees first, and the
contrast validator says the app is clean. `bun run ship-check` being green does
not currently mean this class is clean. That is the finding to act on before any
of the feature work below, because it silently suppresses attention to
everything else.

(Note the validator is partly aware of its own blind spot — it emits a WARN that
it cannot compute the overlap for `components/HelpFab.tsx:175` because the
offset is not a static number — and passes anyway.)

## Verdict

MAGE ID is a genuinely deep construction product with a shallow, leaky seam layer — the engines are better than the app around them. The deterministic core is real and, in places, better than what a $79/mo competitor ships: utils/cpm.ts, utils/wip.ts (locked periods, GAAP loss recognition, profit fade), utils/contractEngine.ts, utils/aiaBilling.ts, utils/subScorecard.ts, utils/estimateCalibration.ts, utils/scheduleColors.ts, the offline write queue, the tenant-cache prefix sweep, and ~30 validate-*.ts guards that encode real product decisions. That is not vibe-coded. But three structural weaknesses run through every dimension I audited. First, the money loop does not close: an approved change order changes the contract total on six read-only surfaces and can never become an invoice line; a sub's paper bill cannot be entered at all (and the RLS has no GC INSERT policy on sub_submitted_invoices, so a UI fix alone would 403); and utils/jobCostEngine.ts books the homeowner's payments TO the GC as job-cost ACTUALS while utils/wip.ts uses the opposite, correct definition — so two screens in one app answer "what has this job cost me" with incompatible arithmetic, and the wrong one feeds the bank-facing WIP tab, the CPI, the margin alerts and the AI. Second, honesty gaps: a failed read renders as "you have no data" (30 swallow-and-serve-cache fallthroughs in ProjectContext), a daily report that fails to email is never persisted while the alert says "saved", and several AI panels print invented dollar benchmarks — journeyman wage rates, "Home Depot" as a supplier name that rides into the client's bid PDF — while the deterministic engine that answers the same question sits uncalled in the repo. Third, the guards lie: I ran validate-contrast.ts and validate-app-slop.ts and both print PASS while six white-on-2.87:1 buttons and two banned purple hexes ship, and validate-type-identity.ts reports PASS at ceiling 0 while 61 screens open in anonymous bold sans. Green CI is currently certifying drift as fixed. None of this is a rewrite — the fixes are mostly small and local, and an unusual share are one-line wiring of things already built.

## The single most valuable fix

Fix what "actual cost" means, then make an approved change order billable. These are one problem wearing two hats and they surfaced in three separate dimensions (money-trust, core-workflow, feature-gaps). utils/jobCostEngine.ts:266-281 sums client Invoice.amountPaid into `actual` and never reads commitment.paidToDate at all (I grepped — jobCostEngine.ts never appears in the paidToDate results), so when the homeowner pays a deposit the job looks like it is burning budget, and a GC who has performed $300k and billed nothing shows Actual $0 and "on track to finish under budget." app/job-costing.tsx:463 states the defect to the user in plain English: "Actual is sum of invoice payments." That one line poisons Job Costing, the Budget Dashboard CPI, the bank-facing WIP tab in Reports (utils/financialReports.ts:100 and :190 call computeJobCost with no receipts and no timeEntries, making `actual` 100% client cash), the Profit report, marginRiskScore, livingEstimate and the homeowner's open-book portal. The paired half: an approved CO folds into contractTotal on six surfaces but has no path to a billable line in app/invoice.tsx, app/bill-from-estimate.tsx or the G703 — and app/cash-flow.tsx:322-324 carries a comment asserting the link exists, which is why the "% of $158,400" mislabel and the week-close routing bug both went unnoticed. Fix order: (1) add `existing.actual += Math.max(0, c.paidToDate ?? 0)` to the commitments loop, THEN delete the invoice-payment loop (deleting it first would zero all subcontractor cost); (2) pass receipts and timeEntries at the two financialReports call sites; (3) add approved-CO rows to bill-from-estimate's sources memo and a "Bill this change order" action on app/change-order.tsx. Everything else in this report is a better product; this is the difference between numbers a contractor can bet his payroll on and numbers he can't.

## Cross-cutting themes

### 1. THE HONESTY GAP — surfaced in 6 of 9 dimensions (empty-loading-error, ai-features, money-trust, field-ergonomics, core-workflow, polish-delight). The app tells the user things that are not true, and it does so in the specific places where trust is cheapest to lose: a failed read renders as 'you have no data'; a daily report that never persisted is announced as 'saved'; EstimateComparison alerts 'Saved' after the write threw; WIP Report reports three successes on an empty account including a bank-reviewable snapshot with nothing in it; an AI briefing invents an overdue count next to the deterministic brief that says all clear; a model's invented 'Home Depot' becomes the supplier name on the client's bid PDF. The pattern is always the same shape — a success path that runs unconditionally, or an error swallowed into an empty array. This is the thing that will lose you a paying contractor, and almost every instance is a trivial or small fix.

### 2. GREEN CI CERTIFYING DRIFT AS FIXED — surfaced in 3 dimensions (visual-craft, polish-delight, onboarding-activation). I ran validate-contrast.ts and validate-app-slop.ts: both print PASS while six white-on-2.87:1 buttons and two of the validator's OWN banned purple hexes ship. validate-type-identity.ts reports PASS at ceiling 0 while 61 screens open in anonymous bold sans. validate-ai-feature-gating.ts's manifest lists one file for a feature with two live call sites. Every failure is the same mechanism: a walk root that names ['app','components'] and skips utils/, or a regex that matches a key name instead of a role, or a list instead of an enumeration. docs/START-HERE.md already states the rule. The guards are a real asset — they just need their blind spots closed before they can be trusted, and right now they are actively suppressing attention.

### 3. MONEY DEFINITIONS DISAGREE ACROSS SCREENS — surfaced in 3 dimensions (money-trust, core-workflow, feature-gaps). 'Actual cost' means client revenue in jobCostEngine and sub payments in wip.ts. 'Billings' includes drafts in one WIP report and excludes them in the other. 'Contract total' includes approved COs on six read-only surfaces and excludes them everywhere a bill is produced. The G702 cover sheet and its own G703 foot to different contract sums. Progress invoices label a percentage of one base and charge a percentage of another. These are not rounding differences — they are different definitions rendered with equal confidence to a banker, a surety, an architect and a homeowner. Pick one definition per term, encode it in a validator, and make every surface derive from it.

### 4. THE ENGINE IS UNCALLED WHERE IT MATTERS MOST — surfaced in 4 dimensions (ai-features, navigation-ia, money-trust, feature-gaps). The pattern repeats with unusual consistency: on the Subs tab where the award is decided, the ungrounded LLM panel is mounted inline and utils/subScorecard.ts is two navigation levels away; the estimate validator scores against 'industry standards' while utils/estimateCalibration.ts holds the GC's own measured bias; the invoice predictor averages every client in the account while paymentPrediction.ts documents the fix; Plan Review cites bare IRC while Code Check resolves the adopted edition one toggle left; Colors.statusFills and barLabelColorFor each have exactly one consumer while an inline palette fails AA. You keep building the correct thing and then not calling it. Wiring these up is the highest ratio of user-visible improvement to engineering hours in the entire report.

### 5. BUILT, DOCUMENTED, AND NOT WIRED — surfaced in 7 of 9 dimensions. 13 features exist in code with no or near-no entry point (listed separately), and in several cases the code's own header comment states the purpose it is failing to serve: useBrainWatch's sourceFailed, OfflineSyncPill, StatusPill, Tutorial's claimed auto-open, LockedAccessCard's field-role copy. The count itself is the signal: this codebase reliably finishes the hard part and skips the last two lines. A recurring 'wire the orphan' pass — or the proposed validate-nav-coverage.ts — would cheaply convert engineering already paid for into product the user can see.

### 6. DESKTOP AND MOBILE ARE MAINTAINED AS SEPARATE PRODUCTS — surfaced in 3 dimensions (navigation-ia, core-workflow, visual-craft). Five hand-maintained nav catalogs, alias routes that break the sidebar's 'you are here' highlight one level in, six project-scoped screens linked bare from the sidebar with no project picker and a dead-end telling the user to navigate elsewhere, and three of eight hidden tabs that never received the back-link fix the other five got. Nothing here is hard; it is all the absence of a single source of truth, which utils/featureRegistry.ts already declares itself to be in its own header comment.
