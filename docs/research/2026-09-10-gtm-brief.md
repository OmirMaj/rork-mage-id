## 2. The three fintech bets

Your billion-dollar strategy doc's premise is also stale in one important way: it treats usage/fintech revenue as the growth engine. ServiceTitan's FY2026 10-K (FYE 2026-01-31) shows usage revenue growing **slower** than subscription in both of the last two years — subscription +28.1% then +25.9%, usage +23.9% then +22.6%; FY26 mix 74.1% subscription / 22.2% usage. Fintech follows distribution. It does not substitute for it.

### B — Payment markup (30–50 bps on Stripe Connect): **OPEN**
**Licensing reality:** clean, and it's the only one that is. `create-payment-link` sets `application_fee_amount` and passes the connected account in the `Stripe-Account` header — a direct charge. The contractor is merchant of record, you never receive payer funds, so there is no money-transmitter exposure and no volume threshold. Fee is computed server-side from `auth.tier`, not the client. `PLATFORM_FEE_BPS` = free 0 / pro 30 / business 50 / enterprise 40.

**But it is scoped to the wrong rail.** `connect-onboarding/index.ts` requests only `capabilities[card_payments][requested]` and `capabilities[transfers][requested]` — verified in code today. Stripe requires `us_bank_account_ach_payments` to be active for ACH debits. So a $100,000 draw costs the contractor ~$2,900 in card fees and will *never* run through MAGE. ACH is 0.8% capped at $5.00. Your addressable volume today is small invoices, not draws.

**First step:** (a) 10 minutes in the Stripe Dashboard — is your platform profile approved to collect application fees, and has *any* connected account ever completed Express onboarding? Nobody has checked. (b) Add the ACH capability to the onboarding request. (c) `marketing/terms.html` contains **zero** fee, application-fee or basis-point language (verified: 0 hits) — you are taking 30–50 bps with no contractual basis. Add the clause, and add the disclosure to `app/aia-pay-app.tsx`, which mints payment links and mentions no fee at all. Your `validate-platform-fees.ts` guard will not catch that omission; it only diffs the tables.

### A — Homeowner financing rev-share: **STRUCTURED, and you have already stepped over the line**
**Licensing reality:** a bare referral — deep-link out, no PII, all underwriting on the partner's page — is a defensible architecture, and your own approved spec (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-05-17-client-financing-design.md`, 2026-05-17) already mandates exactly that, including the "MAGE ID is not a lender" disclosure. Keep that spec; it is stricter and better than most advice you'll get.

Two corrections. **First, there is no 50-state safe harbour for the link-out.** California's only codified finder rule, Cal. Fin. Code §22371, sits inside a small-dollar pilot program that sunsets 2028-01-01, requires a *physical location*, and expressly excludes acting as a finder "merely by providing electronic access to a licensee's website." That is your architecture. Not illegal — unaddressed. **Second, never show a monthly payment figure.** 12 CFR §1026.24(d)(1) makes payment amount a trigger term, pulling in downpayment, repayment terms and APR. Your spec's default of "no number" is correct.

**The live problem:** `app/invoice.tsx` ships today, to users: *"Give the homeowner a monthly option instead of one lump sum. **We pre-fill the application from this invoice.**"* Pre-populating a credit application is not referral — it is the GreenSky fact pattern (CFPB action 2021-07-12: up to $9M redress plus $2.5M penalty, unfairness under the CFPA).

**First step:** delete the words "We pre-fill the application" today. Then one paid hour with a California finance-lending lawyer on one question: *does brokering a bank-exempt lender's consumer loan require a CFL broker licence under Cal. Fin. Code §22050?* That is the load-bearing question and neither you nor I can answer it.

### C — COI re-quote insurance commission: **CLOSED to a solo founder in this form — and you are already soliciting**
**Licensing reality:** Fla. Stat. §626.112(8) and Cal. Ins. Code §1631 both regulate the verb **solicit**. Compensation contingent on a policy being placed is the trip-wire; Florida's statute names the only two exits (non-contingent compensation, or being a licensed insurance agency). And there is no national shortcut: NARAB II was signed 2015-01-12 but its board was never seated, so multi-state producer licensing is still per-state non-resident applications through NIPR. Cost and timeline to license across states: **unknown.** The only verified figure anywhere in the research is California's $188 P&C broker-agent filing fee. Treat any "$5–15K and 3–6 months" estimate as invented.

**The live problem is worse than the bet.** `app/prequal-manager.tsx` ships: *"Compare renewal quotes from 3 brokers — Coterie + Next Insurance + Hiscox quote your sub in 60 seconds."* The component's own header comment concedes no agreement exists. That is a named-carrier solicitation plus false-association exposure on three third-party marks.

**First step:** delete that card this week. If you want the surface, make it non-compensated and say so: "Here are three carriers that write small-contractor GL. We get nothing."
